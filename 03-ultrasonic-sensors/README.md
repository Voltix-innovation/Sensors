
# Ultrasonic Sensors

## 1. Introduction

An ultrasonic sensor uses high-frequency sound waves to detect objects and measure distance. It is commonly used in robotics, obstacle detection, and distance measurement systems.

## 2. Working Principle

The sensor transmits an ultrasonic pulse toward an object. The sound wave reflects from the object and returns as an echo. The sensor measures the time taken by the sound wave to travel to the object and back.

Distance is calculated using:

Distance = (Speed of Sound × Echo Time) / 2

The division by two accounts for the outgoing and returning paths.

## 3. Main Components

- Ultrasonic transmitter
- Ultrasonic receiver
- Control and timing circuit

## 4. Types

- Ultrasonic distance sensors
- Ultrasonic proximity sensors
- Ultrasonic level sensors
- Industrial ultrasonic sensors

## 5. Applications

- Robot obstacle avoidance
- Parking assistance systems
- Liquid-level measurement
- Object detection
- Distance measurement

## 6. Advantages

- Non-contact distance measurement
- Suitable for many indoor applications
- Can detect objects in low-light conditions
- Simple principle of operation

## 7. Limitations

- Soft materials may absorb sound.
- Angled surfaces may reflect sound away from the receiver.
- Temperature and environmental conditions affect sound speed.
- Performance may be reduced by wind, noise, or unsuitable target surfaces.

## 8. Examples

HC-SR04 is a widely used ultrasonic distance sensor for educational projects. Industrial models are available for distance and level measurement.
